Professional Profile and Nursing Identity

My name is Hanna Adhanom. I am a Registered Practical Nurse (RPN) and a Semester 6 Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN) bridging student at Seneca Polytechnic. I currently work on the A3 Stroke Unit at Queensway Carleton Hospital and in long-term care with the City of Ottawa. I am continuing my nursing education because I want to strengthen my clinical judgment, expand my knowledge, and prepare for the increased responsibilities of Registered Nurse (RN) practice.

Working with patients who are recovering from a stroke and with older adults in long-term care has shaped the way I understand nursing. Some patients require close monitoring and rehabilitation, while others live with chronic illness, reduced mobility, or changes in cognition. These settings have taught me that effective nursing care starts with knowing the person, understanding their baseline, and noticing even small changes in their physical or emotional condition.

★

Core Nursing Values

Compassion, respect, dignity, safety, and advocacy are the values that guide my practice. I believe every patient deserves to feel heard and involved in their care, regardless of age, diagnosis, communication ability, or level of independence. I try to explain what I am doing, listen without judgment, protect privacy, and work with patients and families to support realistic goals. I also recognize that a person's mental health is part of their overall health, not something separate from physical illness.

Reflective Analysis

These comments made me reflect on the difference between knowing how to complete a task and understanding when it is necessary, how it should be prioritized, and what requires escalation. During my medicine rotation, preparing for care meant reviewing a patient's medical history, medications, laboratory results, and assessment findings rather than treating each task separately. In my current neurological rehabilitation placement, a patient reported burning with urination; I communicated the concern to the care team, and a urinalysis was ordered. This reinforced my responsibility to take patient-reported symptoms seriously, collect relevant data, communicate promptly, and follow up on the plan of care.

My next step is to make my progress observable. On each clinical day, I will identify the highest-priority concerns for my assigned patients, explain the rationale for interventions during discussion with my instructor, and reflect on one clinical decision afterward. I will continue requesting feedback about assessment, escalation, organization, documentation, and handover, then use that feedback to adjust my next shift. This process supports clinical judgment, professional accountability, and the self-awareness expected in RN practice.

Professional Standards and Regulations — Mental Health Nursing Context

I selected three College of Nurses of Ontario (CNO) Registered Nurse entry-to-practice competencies that are relevant to my work in stroke care, long-term care, medicine, and ABI rehabilitation. Together, these competencies connect mental health-informed care, therapeutic relationships, and professional accountability across clinical settings.

Competency 1.19

Incorporating Mental Health Promotion in Nursing Care

Description

This CNO entry-to-practice competency requires nurses to incorporate mental health promotion when providing nursing care, recognizing that emotional well-being is integral to overall health in every care setting (CNO, 2023).

Evidence from Practice

Patients recovering from stroke or brain injury may be coping with sudden loss of independence, anxiety, grief, or uncertainty. On the A3 Stroke Unit and in neurological rehabilitation, I try to notice emotional responses alongside physical findings and respond with calm reassurance, clear explanations, and appropriate referral of concerns.

Competency 3.5

Using Relational Practice to Adapt Communication Skills

Description

This competency requires nurses to use relational practice to adapt communication skills to the needs of each patient, promoting trust, participation, and therapeutic nurse–client relationships (CNO, 2023).

Evidence from Practice

I adjust communication when a patient needs additional response time or has difficulty expressing needs. These approaches promote trust and help patients participate in decisions about their care rather than being treated as passive recipients. This is particularly relevant in neuro rehabilitation where patients may have cognitive or communication changes.

Competency 2.9

Self-Assessment and Professional Growth Planning

Description

This competency concerns self-assessment, identifying learning needs, developing learning plans, and evaluating professional growth as part of ongoing accountability to nursing standards (CNO, 2023).

Evidence from Practice

I use written clinical evaluations, guided reflection, and feedback to identify strengths and areas requiring more development. My BSN 525 instructor recognized improvements in comprehensive assessment and sound clinical judgment, and I am applying this feedback during BSN 625 by preparing before shifts and organizing care for two patients.

5C Mental Health–Informed Analysis

Therapeutic Communication

Listening, asking questions at an appropriate pace, using an encouraging tone, and confirming understanding allows me to identify barriers and respond more respectfully. This is especially important in long-term care, where loneliness, grief, or changes in cognition can affect participation in care.

Psychosocial & Emotional Assessment

A person with stroke or acquired brain injury who appears withdrawn or refuses an activity may be frightened, fatigued, overwhelmed, depressed, or unable to communicate clearly. Rather than labelling the person as unmotivated, I assess pain, cognition, emotional distress, environmental triggers, and preferences.

Trauma-Informed / Recovery-Oriented Care

Explaining procedures, seeking consent, offering manageable choices, respecting privacy, and collaborating with patients can reduce feelings of lost control during rehabilitation or personal care. Recovery-oriented nursing means recognizing strengths, setting realistic goals with patients, and supporting hope and autonomy even when full physical recovery is uncertain.

Professional Self-Awareness & Boundaries

If someone expresses frustration or declines assistance, I must manage my own emotional reaction, maintain boundaries, and reflect rather than respond defensively. Professional self-awareness is essential to providing consistent, compassionate care across complex clinical situations.

5D Reflective Synthesis

Before studying mental health nursing, I often thought about mental health care mainly in relation to psychiatric diagnoses. My learning has broadened that view. I now recognize that emotional distress, coping, loss, hope, and the need to feel in control can influence care on a stroke unit, a medicine floor, in neurological rehabilitation, or in long-term care. Mental health knowledge helps me see the whole person, not only the condition that brought them into care.

As I prepare for RN practice, I want to be more deliberate in assessing emotional concerns, adapting communication, and recognizing my own assumptions. I will continue to use feedback, reflection, and the CNO standards to guide my decisions. These lessons support my development as a nurse who can combine sound assessment and clinical judgment with compassion, professional boundaries, and respect for each patient's choices.
